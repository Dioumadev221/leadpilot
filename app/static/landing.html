The problem

Where inbound deals slip

Forms, Upwork, LinkedIn and referrals land in one inbox. Someone has to read each message, look up the company, decide, copy it into the CRM and write back. That is where deals slip.

01

The best lead waits behind the noise

Vendor pitches and newsletters bury the one enquiry with a budget. By the time someone triages, the buyer has booked a call elsewhere.

02

The CRM fills with junk

SEO offers and test submissions get copied in next to real buyers, and every pipeline number built on that data is wrong.

03

Nobody trusts the score

A model that says 82 today and 64 tomorrow for the same lead gets ignored. Sales goes back to gut feeling.

The pipeline

A fixed path, not an open loop

Every lead takes the same route through a bounded LangGraph graph. Two model calls per lead at most, so cost is known up front and behaviour is testable.

1

Capture

Webhook for any form tool, REST API or manual entry. Duplicates dropped by idempotency key.

POST /api/webhooks/lead
2

Enrich

Business, free or disposable email. Company website title and description, fetched safely.

public hosts only
3

Qualify

An agent extracts intent, ICP fit, budget, timeline and use case as typed data, quoting the lead.

structured output
4

Score

A rule table turns signals into 0–100 with a breakdown. Spam stops here.

hot ≥ 70 · warm ≥ 45
5

Approve

A writer agent drafts the CRM note and reply. You approve, the contact lands in your CRM.

human in the loop
Control

The model reads. The rules score. You decide.

Language models are good at understanding messy messages. They are bad at being consistent. LeadPilot keeps them on the first job only.

  • Same lead, same score. Points come from rules you can read and tune, not from a prompt.
  • Approval before sync. Nothing reaches the CRM until a person approves, and the draft can be edited first.
  • Full audit trail. Each agent step, its duration and every human decision is logged per lead.
  • Keeps running. If the model provider fails, the pipeline falls back to rules and flags the lead.

Engineering

Built to run, not to demo

Python, FastAPI, LangGraph and PostgreSQL. The live demo is the production code on a sample workspace.

graph.pyBounded agent graph

Four steps at most with conditional routing. No agent can loop and burn tokens.

agents.pyTyped outputs

Every agent returns a validated schema. The pipeline never parses free text from a model.

services.pyPostgres job queue

Atomic claims, capped retries, failed state with the error kept for inspection.

crm/CRM adapters

HubSpot and Zoho with rate-limit retries. Adding a CRM is one adapter file.

enrichment.pySafe enrichment

Website lookups only reach public addresses, with every redirect checked.

tests/Tested in CI

Routing, scoring, CRM request shapes and the approval flow, on SQLite and PostgreSQL.

FAQ

What teams ask first

Does the AI write directly into our CRM?

No. The agents qualify, score and draft. A person approves each lead, optionally after editing the note or reply, and only then is the contact created or updated in HubSpot or Zoho.

Can it use our own definition of a good lead?

Yes. Your ideal customer profile is written in plain language for the qualifier agent, and the scoring rules and hot/warm thresholds are configuration, not prompts.

Which models does it use, and what does a lead cost?

OpenAI, Anthropic, or any OpenAI-compatible endpoint such as Groq, Gemini or a self-hosted model. Each lead costs two model calls; with a small model that is a fraction of a cent.

Where does our data live?

In your own PostgreSQL database and your own cloud account. LeadPilot ships as a Docker image and also runs serverless.

What happens if the model provider is down?

The qualifier and writer fall back to deterministic rules and templates, the lead is flagged with a warning, and nothing is lost.

Is the live demo real?

The pipeline is the production code. The workspace, Northwind Studio, is sample data, syncs to a sandbox CRM and resets every day. Send it a lead of your own and watch it go through.
